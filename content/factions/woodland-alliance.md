## Overview

As the Woodland Alliance, your aim isn't to establish strongholds or raise armies, but to spread <term>sympathy</term> for your revolutionary cause among the denizens of the Woodland. To spread your sympathy, you'll need the aid of advocates from your <term>supporter deck</term>, a small deck of cards on your faction board separate from your hand cards that is limited to five cards until you establish yourself more firmly in the Woodland. Once you **Mobilize** cards from your hand to your supporter deck, you can spend those cards to garner support in new clearings, placing sympathy tokens and scoring increasing victory points for each token placed. While your first sympathy token can be placed anywhere on the map, subsequent tokens must be placed in new clearings connected to existing sympathetic clearings. Additionally, the cost to place new sympathy tokens increases as your cause grows, and the presence of opponent's warriors can trigger <term>Martial Law</term>, further increasing the cost.

Your sympathy tokens also impede your opponents as sympathetic clearings become intolerant of their military actions. Whenever an opponent moves warriors into a clearing with a sympathy token or removes the sympathy token (such as by battling), they spark <term>outrage</term>, forcing them to pay a card from their hand matching the clearing's suit into your supporter deck. If they can't pay, they show you their hand as proof, and you draw the top card of the deck and place it in your supporter deck.

With both sympathy and support, you can leverage your influence to a more destructive end: <term>revolt</term>. You can only revolt in a clearing that already has a sympathy token, but when you do, it immediately removes all non-Woodland Alliance pieces from that clearing and establishes a <term>base</term> there, your foothold in the Woodland for more militaristic action. You only have three bases total, one for each of the three clearing suits, but having a base on the map lifts the five-card limit on your supporter deck.

Bases also allow you to **Train** <term>officers</term>, warriors placed on your faction board that allow you to take actions such as **Recruit**, placing warriors at a base, and **Organize**, allowing you to replace a warrior with a sympathy token in a clearing without one, bypassing the supporter cost. Once you have warriors on the board, your skill in <term>Guerrilla War</term> allows you to take the higher roll as the defender in battles, making your warriors very difficult to remove.

## Strategy

As the Woodland Alliance, your strategy is both singular and straightforward: place sympathy tokens. Your track of sympathy tokens alone is worth 22 victory points, which doesn't account for points gained from sympathy tokens that are inevitably removed and replaced, or points gained from pieces removed during a revolt. Given that, your strategy should focus on always being able to continue placing sympathy tokens.

### The Opening

Starting the game with nothing on the board keeps the Woodland Alliance's opening fairly standard: use the three starting cards in your supporter deck to place three sympathy tokens on the map, then Mobilize the three starting cards in your hand to your supporter deck. Finally, on the following turn, spend two of those supporter cards to revolt in one of the three clearings you placed sympathy tokens in to establish your first base.

This opening is more nuanced than it sounds, however, and it immediately confronts you with the core question of the Woodland Alliance, which is where to place your sympathy tokens. It can be tempting to place your tokens in very central clearings, but a particularly inconvenient sympathy token will probably get removed by an opponent who also wants a centralized position. On the other hand, a token that is too safely out of the way also means a base that is too out of the way to effectively spread sympathy later in the game. It's a balancing act that's determined in no small part by how aggressive the other players at the table are. The worst outcome, however, is to have all three sympathy tokens removed before your second turn, which will effectively put you back at square one and a turn behind everyone else, so be wary of playing too riskily.

Once you've established your first base, your supporter deck is no longer capped at five cards and the rest of the game becomes more psychological in terms of placing sympathy tokens. Be wary of the middle, two-cost section of your Sympathy track however, as this is where the victory points from placing the sympathy least outweighs the cost of placing it, and opponents will try to keep you in that section. Additionally, you'll want to quickly establish a second base that is two or more clearings away from the first base, as this will extend your reach across most of the board. Bases also allow you to spread more sympathy with Organize, allowing you to save your supporter cards for clearings further from your bases. Be careful not to train too many officers, however, as warriors in your Officers box are warriors that aren't on the board.

As an aside, the Woodland Alliance can be oppressive at tables that are new to the game, and while part of that comes from players still learning how to disrupt the Woodland Alliance's scoring engine, it also often comes from players misunderstanding the faction's rules. The ability to directly battle sympathy tokens and score a victory point for removing them, as well as the Martial Law penalty on the cost of placing sympathy tokens, are often unknown or overlooked by new tables and worth a reminder if they seem to be struggling against the faction.

### The Win

The Woodland Alliance has one of the strongest victory point bursts of all the factions in the game, evidenced by the oft-cited wisdom that you should not cross 20 victory points except on the turn where you plan to reach 30 victory points and win. This is entirely plausible since the last three tokens on your Sympathy track alone are worth 12 victory points, meaning you can go from 18 points to victory with as little as three officers, nine supporter cards, or some combination thereof.

The key to this kind of victory is in the setup beforehand, but even that differs little from the Woodland Alliance's strategy for the rest of the game. Defending your bases is the biggest concern, as losing one will slash both your officers and your supporters, leaving you without the actions to close out the game. While most opponents won't want to dedicate the warriors or actions necessary to remove a base, an opponent whose only alternative is losing certainly will, and this goes doubly for factions that can remove your bases more easily such as the <ql>[Lizard Cult](@/factions/lizard-cult.md)</ql> or the <ql>[Lord of the Hundreds](@/factions/lord-of-the-hundreds.md)</ql>.
